INTRODUCTIONThe human genome harbors far more non-coding transcripts than protein-coding genes, with these non-coding RNAs acting as critical regulators of gene expression. Notably, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) represent a particularly versatile and rapidly evolving class. While traditionally defined as transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides, recent consensus recommendations suggest a 500-nucleotide threshold to better distinguish lncRNAs from small regulatory RNAs and most infrastructural RNAs (1).Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are transcribed from diverse genomic regions, including intergenic loci, sequences within or adjacent to protein-coding genes in the sense or antisense orientation, and regulatory elements, such as enhancers (1). Current annotations identify over 30,000 human lncRNA loci, though the complete repertoire likely extends much further (1, 2). While typically transcribed by RNA polymerase II like mRNAs, lncRNAs display substantial variability in RNA processing. Some undergo canonical capping and splicing, whereas others are produced via non-conventional pathways, resulting in heterogeneous transcript populations (3, 4). Structurally, lncRNAs often exhibit modular architecture, with distinct domains mediating chromatin association, protein interactions, or nuclear localization. Xist, a master regulator lncRNA of X chromosome inactivation (XCI), exemplifies this modularity, facilitating the integration of multiple regulatory pathways (5, 6).One of the defining characteristics of lncRNAs is their high tissue- and cell-type- specific expression (7-10), with the majority of human lncRNAs being tissue-specific, far exceeding that observed for protein-coding genes (8). Brain and testes show particularly high enrichment of tissue-specific lncRNAs (8, 9). Single-cell studies further demonstrate that many lncRNAs are expressed in a highly restricted cell-type manner, particularly in the developing human brain, although this work was limited to polyadenylated transcripts (10). Recent advances in single-cell total RNA sequencing enable the capture of both polyadenylated and non-adenylated lncRNAs, providing a more comprehensive view of cell-type-specific lncRNA expression (11, 12).Comparative genomic and transcriptomic analyses indicate that most lncRNAs are species- and primate-specific (7, 13, 14), suggesting that lncRNAs may play a role in species-specific evolutionary changes in gene regulatory programs. Alongside their potential divergent functions, some lncRNAs are functionally conserved, notwithstanding their limited sequence conservation. Recent studies have shown that despite limited sequence homology, mouse Snhg9 and human SNHG9 share conserved molecular mechanisms (15, 16). In both species, the gut microbiota suppresses Snhg9/SNHG9 expression, which normally sequesters CCAR2 and thereby releases SIRT1. In mouse intestinal metabolism, released SIRT1 represses PPARγ, a critical regulator of lipid metabolism, showing that the microbiota−Snhg9 axis controls lipid homeostasis and metabolic disease susceptibility (15). In cancer, Snhg9 and SNHG9 similarly promote tumor growth by enabling SIRT1-dependent repression of the tumor suppressor p53, showing that microbiota-mediated suppression of this lncRNA protects against colorectal tumorigenesis (16). These findings highlight the conserved molecular mechanism of Snhg9/SNHG9 across species and its context-dependent physiological roles.Mechanistically, lncRNAs control gene expression through diverse mechanisms that act in cis or trans (Fig. 1A-D). Cis-acting lncRNAs influence nearby genes on the same chromosome, often by modulating transcription at adjacent promoters and enhancer–promoter interaction. Trans-acting lncRNAs regulate distant genes, including on different chromosomes, by acting as diffusible molecules. Across both modes, lncRNAs can recruit or evict their interacting proteins, including chromatin modifying complexes and transcription factors, at specific target loci. LncRNAs can also bind DNA directly to form RNA−DNA triplex or R-loop structures. The specific molecular details of these mechanisms are discussed below.This review explores these molecular mechanisms underlying lncRNA-mediated gene regulation, with particular focus on their roles in chromatin regulation and spatial genome organization in the nucleus. Their high cell-type- or tissue-specific expression patterns establish context-dependent epigenetic modifications and spatial architecture, thereby coordinating the fine-tuned regulation of gene expression at multiple levels. Consequently, dysregulation of lncRNA function leads to developmental abnormalities and complex diseases, including cancers and neurodevelopmental disorders. We highlight how these properties enable lncRNAs to orchestrate complex regulatory programs underlying development, adult physiology, and pathological processes.REGULATION OF EPIGENETIC MODIFICATIONS BY LNCRNASLncRNAs have emerged as central players in epigenetic modifications across diverse biological processes, including development, lineage commitment, and tumorigenesis. In this section, we specifically focus on their roles in regulating histone modification and DNA methylation.Histone modificationLncRNAs regulate histone modifications through distinct molecular mechanisms. These include modulation of chromatin-modifying complexes such as Polycomb repressive complexes 1 and 2 (PRC1 and PRC2) or activating Trithorax group/Mixed-lineage leukemia (TrxG/MLL) complexes, coordination of opposing enzymatic activities for context-dependent regulation, or direct DNA-binding through triplex or R-loop structures.LncRNAs can function through repressive mechanisms to silence target genes. Once characterized as a trans-acting lncRNA (17), HOTAIR interacts with both PRC2 and LSD1 through its 5’ and 3’ domains, respectively, to coordinate H3K27me3 deposition and H3K4me2 demethylation for robust genome wide gene silencing (18). Recently, HOTAIR−EZH2 (a catalytic component of PRC2) interaction has been shown to extend to the tumor suppressor p53’s target genes: the EZH2−HOTAIR−PURB complex is recruited to the p21 promoter, resulting in transcriptional repression (19). Braveheart (Bvht) serves as a critical regulator of cardiovascular lineage specification, partly through its association with SUZ12, a PRC2 subunit (20). In genomic imprinting, Kcnq1ot1 establishes cis-acting, lineage-specific imprinting by recruiting PRC2 and the histone methyltransferase G9a that mediates repressive H3K9 methylation in the placenta (21). Similarly, Air (Airn) maintains imprinting by directing G9a to its target promoter (22). ANRIL recruits PRC1 and PRC2 to mediate transcriptional repression of the INK4b/ARF/INK4a tumor suppressor locus in cis through its antisense orientation (23, 24).While these examples show lncRNA-mediated PRC2 recruitment, PRC2−RNA interactions may be more complex than simple scaffolding (25-27). PRC2 binds RNA G-quadruplex (rG4) structures with high affinity, yet this binding inhibits rather than activates its methyltransferase activity (28, 29). rG4 unfolding relieves this inhibition and releases PRC2 onto chromatin for H3K27me3 deposition (29). The extent of direct PRC2−RNA binding in vivo remains debated, as a stringent denaturing purification method failed to recover PRC2−RNA complexes (27, 30). However, convergent evidence from biochemical, genetic, and small molecule studies of Xist−PRC2 interactions (discussed below) and from cryo-EM structures of PRC2−rG4 complexes supports functional RNA-directed PRC2 regulation (25, 26). Further investigation is needed to fully elucidate PRC2−RNA mechanisms in chromatin regulation.LncRNAs can function through activating mechanisms to promote gene expression. Through chromosomal looping, HOTTIP is positioned near the 5’HOXA locus, where it recruits WDR5/MLL1 complex and consequent gene activation, contributing to body pattern formation (31). LncPRESS1 maintains pluripotency in embryonic stem cells by sequestering the histone deacetylase SIRT6 from pluripotency gene promoters, thereby preserving H3K9ac and H3K56ac modifications that promote gene expression (32).Some lncRNAs demonstrate dual regulatory functions by coordinating both repressive and activating chromatin modifiers, or through isoform-specific mechanisms. Fendrr interacts with both PRC2 and TrxG/MLL complexes to regulate development of lateral mesoderm-derived tissues, including heart and body wall (33). HOTAIRM1 coordinates sequential HOXA activation through chromatin domain separation and isoform-specific protein association: unspliced transcripts recruit the UTX/MLL complex to activate proximal genes, while spliced transcripts interact with PRC2 to repress central genes (34).In addition, lncRNAs employ direct DNA-binding mechanisms to target chromatin modifiers. MEG3 forms RNA−DNA triplex structures in parallel with its binding to EZH2/PRC2 complex, thereby guiding the complex to TGF-β pathway genes to repress transcription (35). Similarly, HOTAIR can form the RNA−DNA triplex at target loci, while HOTAIR recruits both PRC2 and LSD1 (36). PARTICLE, induced by low-dose radiation and transcribed antisense to MAT2A, forms a promoter-proximal DNA−RNA triplex at the MAT2A locus and represses MAT2A, partly via interaction with SUZ12/PRC2 complex, contributing to methylation homeostasis during radiation response (37). In addition to RNA−DNA triplex formation, lncRNAs can form R-loops, three-stranded nucleic acid structures in which RNA displaces one DNA strand. By forming R-loops at target gene loci, APOLO decoys LHP1 (a component of plant PRC1) from chromatin, thereby coordinating auxin-responsive genes during Arabidopsis lateral root development in trans (38).DNA methylation LncRNAs can control DNA methylation patterns through various mechanisms, including sequestration or recruitment of DNA methyltransferases and R-loop-mediated targeting of DNA demethylases.RNA association with the DNA methyltransferase DNMT1 was shown to be widespread in the genome. Among them, extra-coding CEBPA (ecCEBPA), which encompasses the CEBPA locus, binds and sequesters DNMT1, thereby protecting the CEBPA promoter from methylation-induced silencing (39). Conversely, lncRNAs can recruit DNA methyltransferases. During myogenesis, Dum utilizes intrachromosomal looping to recruit DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B to the distal Dppa2 promoter, enforcing CpG methylation and gene silencing that ensure proper myoblast differentiation and promote efficient muscle regeneration (40).In addition to modulation of DNA methyltransferases, lncRNAs can form R-loops that recruit DNA demethylases to promote targeted demethylation. TARID is transcribed antisense to the tumor suppressor TCF21, which is frequently hypermethylated and silenced in cancer (41). By forming an R-loop at the TCF21 promoter, TARID recruits GADD45A, which interacts with both the DNA repair machinery TDG and the DNA demethylase TET1, resulting in CpG demethylation and TCF21 reactivation (41, 42). Notably, genome-wide analyses reveal that TET1 binds CpG islands (CGIs) in an R-loop-dependent manner, suggesting a general mechanism by which lncRNA-induced R-loops guide TET1 to CGI promoters (42).These epigenetic mechanisms cooperate with spatial genome organizations to achieve comprehensive transcriptional control. In the following section, we explore how lncRNAs contribute to chromatin folding and nuclear compartmentalization.REGULATION OF 3D GENOME ARCHITECTURE BY LNCRNASThe mammalian genome is spatially organized at multiple levels. Approximately two meters of DNA is compacted into a nucleus only a few micrometers in diameter through hierarchical folding (Fig. 2A): from fine-scale chromatin loops to topologically associating domains (TADs) or contact domains, to higher-order A and B compartments representing transcriptionally active and inactive regions, respectively, and finally to chromosome territories (43). The nucleus also contains various membrane-less compartments (44, 45). Importantly, the chromatin loops and nuclear compartments undergo highly dynamic changes that are tightly linked to spatiotemporal gene regulation (45, 46). Together with their highly context-dependent expression, lncRNA-mediated modulation of genome architecture facilitates dynamic transcriptional programs and continuous reorganization of chromatin loops and nuclear compartments in response to developmental cues, cellular signaling, and environmental stress.One key insight into lncRNAs’ role in chromatin architecture comes from their association with CTCF, originally known as a transcription factor, and now recognized as an architectural protein that anchors chromatin loops. CTCF−RNA interactions have emerged as an additional regulatory layer for CTCF function. Studies have revealed that CTCF has a large RNA interactome (47, 48), and mutations of its RNA-binding region (RBR) influence the formation of topological domains (49). Among CTCF-interacting lncRNAs, Jpx directly modulates chromatin loops genome-wide by selectively displacing CTCF from low-affinity sites during mouse embryonic stem cell differentiation (Fig. 2B) (50). While Jpx acts in trans, CCAT1-L acts in cis to facilitate CTCF recruitment to target sites. CCAT1-L is transcribed from a super-enhancer upstream of MYC in colorectal cancer and facilitates CTCF-dependent chromatin looping at the MYC locus, illustrating architectural remodeling in tumorigenesis (Fig. 2C) (51).The modulation of promoter−enhancer contacts by lncRNAs employs diverse context-dependent mechanisms. CCAT1-5L maintains loops between the CCAT1-5L, MYC, and PVT1 loci through interactions with MYC promoter RNA and MYC enhancer RNA (PVT1), as well as HNRNPK protein (Fig. 2C) (52). ThymoD expression results in the demethylation of CTCF sites and recruitment of cohesin, thereby promoting CTCF/cohesin-dependent enhancer−promoter looping interactions, and repositioning the Bcl11b enhancer from heterochromatin to euchromatin during T-cell lineage commitment (53). UMLILO, transcribed from a super-enhancer, facilitates H3K4me3 epigenetic priming of CXCL chemokine genes by recruiting WDR5−MLL1 complex to their promoters in cis within a single TAD, where chromatin looping brings them into spatial proximity. The functional substitution of UMLILO by HOTTIP and further evidence demonstrate that lncRNA−WDR5 interactions constitute a mechanism for H3K4me3-mediated epigenetic priming in trained immunity (54). Meg3 represents a distinct mechanism whereby it interferes with promoter−enhancer loops, resulting in the downregulation of metabolic genes. This activity is normally abolished by Meg3’s sequestration in β-actin-containing ribonucleoprotein complexes away from chromatin (55).R-loop formation by lncRNAs also regulates chromatin architecture. HOTTIP-mediated R-loops stabilize CTCF and cohesin binding, promoting TAD formation and oncogenic transcription (56).Furthermore, lncRNAs control higher-order nuclear architecture through multiple mechanisms (Fig. 2D, E). Firre mediates inter-chromosomal interactions by interacting with the nuclear matrix protein HNRNPU (57). Other lncRNAs form and regulate nuclear compartmentalization via LLPS (liquid–liquid phase separation) (58). DIGIT promotes phase-separated condensate formation through interaction with BRD3 protein, thereby regulating transcription factors that are critical for endoderm differentiation (59). LoNA is critical for nucleolus formation through phase separation mechanisms, controlling gene expression programs during the two-cell stage of embryos (60). SLERT ensures proper liquidity of nucleolar subcompartments by acting as an RNA chaperone that modulates the conformation of DDX21 RNA helicase to promote RNA polymerase I transcription (61). MALAT1 and NEAT1 associate with nuclear speckles and paraspeckles, respectively (1, 4). NEAT1_2, the long NEAT1 isoform, functions as an essential structural scaffold for paraspeckles by promoting phase separation through the recruitment of multivalent proteins, such as NONO and SFPQ (62). Genome-wide chromatin-binding maps reveal that NEAT1 and MALAT1 preferentially bind active chromatin sites across the genome, suggesting a functional and structural link between actively transcribed genes and nuclear speckles/paraspeckles (63). Both lncRNAs exhibit dynamic, stress-responsive behavior. NEAT1 regulates paraspeckle formation and dynamics in response to innate immune stimuli (64) or genotoxic stress (65). MALAT1 translocates from nuclear speckles to phase-separated nuclear bodies upon heat shock (66). These dynamics demonstrate stimulus-responsive remodeling of lncRNA-containing compartments.LncRNAs can also form compartments through high local concentration-driven processes that shape long-range DNA interactions and recruit regulatory molecules (45). This mechanism is exemplified by Kcnq1ot1, which recruits SPEN (SHARP) and HDAC3 to silence the target genes within spatially confined regions (67).Collectively, lncRNAs operate across multiple architectural scales to regulate dynamic nuclear reorganization, enabling context-specific transcriptional programs.X CHROMOSOME INACTIVATION (XCI): AN INTEGRATIVE MODEL OF LNCRNA FUNCTION IN EPIGENETIC AND SPATIAL REGULATIONLncRNA research has been significantly advanced through extensive studies of X chromosome inactivation (XCI). XCI is an elegant epigenetic process that occurs early in mammalian female development to equalize X-linked gene expression between females and males. XCI is primarily regulated by lncRNAs, including Xist, Jpx, Ftx, Tsix, Xite, and Linx (see comprehensive reviews for details elsewhere) (5, 6). XCI studies have provided crucial mechanistic insights into lncRNA functionality, using powerful model systems, particularly mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs). Beyond its role in chromosome dosage compensation, XCI serves as a model for lncRNA-mediated epigenetic regulation more broadly, across multiple dimensions from linear chromatin modifications to three-dimensional genome organization (Fig. 3A-C).Xist Xist initiates XCI by coating the inactive X chromosome in cis, thereby inducing chromosome-wide silencing through integrated epigenetic and spatial mechanisms (5, 6). Once tethered to the nucleation site through its interaction with YY1 (68), Xist spreads across the X chromosome through a sequential, two-step mechanism, initially localizing to gene-rich domains before expanding into gene-poor regions, as demonstrated by Xist’s chromatin binding maps (69). This spreading exploits existing 3D chromosome architecture for proximity-dependent targeting, while reciprocally modifying chromosome conformations (70).Furthermore, Xist repels architectural proteins such as cohesins, actively shaping the inactive X chromosome (Xi) conformation (71). Indeed, Xist RNA directs architectural changes of the Xi, as deleting Xist results in conformations reminiscent of the active X chromosome (Xa) (71, 72). Unlike the Xa or autosomes, the Xi is partitioned into two megadomains by the boundary Dxz4, with attenuated TADs and absent A/B compartments (6). During XCI, A/B compartments are reorganized into Xi-specific S1/S2 compartments, a process dependent on Xist RNA. Xist recruits PRC1 via HNRNPK. This process then forms S1/S2 compartments and deposits H2AK119ub1 marks that enable SMCHD1 recruitment. SMCHD1 then merges S1/S2 compartments into the Xi structure (73-75).Studies of Xist–chromatin interactions, enabled by breakthrough advances in RNA-centric methods, have been extended to the identification of Xist’s protein interactome (71, 76, 77). These studies collectively identified a diverse repertoire of Xist-interacting proteins that act through distinct molecular pathways, including chromatin modification/remodeling, chromosome architecture, m6A RNA methylation, and nuclear matrix association. Underlying these diverse functions, different repeat domains (Repeats A to F) within Xist mediate distinct protein interactions. Repeat A (RepA) binds SPEN (SHARP), which in turn recruits the SMRT co-repressor complex, resulting in HDAC3 activation and subsequent histone deacetylation (76, 77). While RepA recruits SPEN and PRC2 (71), Repeat B (RepB) binds HNRNPK to recruit PRC1/2 (78, 79). Both pathways converge to ensure robust PRC2 recruitment and widespread H3K27me3 deposition (80). Repeat E (RepE) is required for Xist localization through recruitment of the nuclear matrix proteins such as CIZ1 (81, 82), and condensate formation via interactions with PTBP1, MATR3, TDP-43, and CELF1 (83).Xist condensates have been further investigated using super-resolution three-dimensional structured illumination microscopy. Only two Xist RNA molecules per focus (∼50 foci per cell) nucleate supramolecular complexes enriched with silencing and architectural proteins including PRC1 and SPEN, thereby inducing progressive chromatin compaction and consequent gradual gene silencing (84).Most recently, Xist RNA has been shown to employ LLPS mechanism through RepB interaction with HNRNPK to form condensates that confine Xist spreading in cis (85). Once internalized by HNRNPK condensates, RepB enhances LLPS, promoting the recruitment of XCI factors, including PRC1 and SMCHD1, and thereby driving long-range chromatin mixing and compartment merging to promote Xist spreading and gene silencing in cis. RepB and RepE may work together with different interacting proteins to form condensates and confer proper LLPS property to ensure proximity-based spreading and efficient gene silencing (83, 85).Although Xist-driven silencing reshapes the Xi into a globally repressive structure, a subset of genes on the Xi, termed escapee, remain active, varying across tissues, developmental stages, and ages (86). They are frequently located within distinct 3D domains (6), suggesting that 3D genome architecture could selectively override epigenetic silencing.LncRNAs as activators or repressors of XistMultiple lncRNAs regulate Xist through opposing positive and negative pathways. The negative pathway includes Tsix, Xite, and Linx, while the positive pathway includes Jpx, Ftx, and Xert (5, 6). These major lncRNAs are transcribed from the X-inactivation center (Xic), which are organized into two separate TADs, each containing the negative and positive pathways (Fig. 3B, C) (5, 6). Negative Xist regulators exist within anti-XCI domain (Tsix TAD), while positive Xist regulators are present within the pro-XCI domain (Xist TAD). During XCI, these two TADs undergo coordinated changes: Xist–Jpx–Ftx interactions strengthen, while Tsix–Xite interactions weaken (5, 6, 87).Early genetic studies using mouse embryonic stem cells identified Tsix, an antisense transcript to Xist, as a key repressor of Xist. Deleting Tsix on one X chromosome designates that allele as the future inactive X, demonstrating that Tsix and Xist expression is mutually exclusive (88). Tsix regulates Xist through chromatin modifications, including H3K9me2 deposition via G9a (89, 90). Xite functions as an enhancer for Tsix expression and persistence on the future Xa (91). Both Tsix and Xite RNAs ensure mutually exclusive choice between the two X chromosomes by recruiting CTCF to facilitate X−X chromosome pairing (47). Originally identified as an 80 kb lncRNA, Linx negatively regulates Xist expression through its promoter region (LinxP), rather than its transcript or transcription (92).Xist activation requires a positive regulator, in addition to loss of the Tsix-mediated repressive effect. Indeed, Tsix-mediated repression is not the sole mechanism of Xist regulation, and Jpx is an essential bona fide activator. Jpx activates Xist transcription by evicting CTCF from the Xist promoter, thus relieving repression (93). A recent study further demonstrates that Jpx-directed CTCF displacement extends to the Tsix and Xite loci, reconfiguring functional loops within Xic, and thereby coordinating Xist upregulation and Tsix downregulation in cis (87). Also, both Ftx and Xert activate Xist expression in cis. Ftx functions through its transcriptional activity (94), and Xert functions as cis-regulatory elements, with its transcription contributing to Xist activation (95).ROLE OF LNCRNAS IN DISEASEGiven that lncRNAs actively participate in fundamental biological processes, such as development, cell cycle, and immune response, dysregulation of lncRNAs has been associated with various disease contexts. The mechanisms of action of lncRNAs frequently overlap with regulatory pathways that become abnormally rewired in pathogenesis. For example, lncRNAs regulate lineage specification and metabolism during cellular reprogramming through pathways shared with oncogenic processes (96). LncRNA loci often harbor genomic alterations, such as amplifications and single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), resulting in aberrant gene expression in cancers (97). Notably, approximately 22% of lncRNA genes reside in genomic regions with focal somatic copy-number alterations associated with various tumors (98).Integrated approaches, including expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) analysis, co-expression networks, and genome-wide association study (GWAS)–QTL colocalization, have linked lncRNAs to inflammatory bowel disease, diabetes, coronary artery disease, lupus, and multiple sclerosis (99). For example, LINC01475 and RP11-129J12.1 act in colon and spleen tissues to influence ulcerative colitis susceptibility, illustrating how lncRNA genetic variations contribute to pathogenesis in specific cellular contexts (99).Xist/XIST in pathogenesisXist/XIST loss or dysregulation contributes to multiple pathological conditions, including cancer and female-biased autoimmune diseases, through the disruption of XCI. Xist/XIST is therefore critical for XCI maintenance and cellular homeostasis throughout life as well as initial XCI establishment. Conditional Xist deletion in mouse hematopoietic stem cells reactivates the Xi, leading to genome-wide transcriptional dysregulation and aggressive myeloproliferative neoplasm/myelodysplastic syndrome with 100% female penetrance (100). Female tumors, particularly breast and ovarian cancers, often lose the condensed heterochromatin structure of the Xi, termed the Barr body, suggesting a link between XCI maintenance failure and tumorigenesis (101). Consistently, XIST depletion in human mammary epithelial stem cells (MaSC) impairs normal differentiation and increases the oncogenic potential, leading to highly aggressive tumors (102). This occurs through partial Xi reactivation causing MED14 upregulation, which enhances enhancer activity and transcriptional programs. XIST deficiency and Xi instability correlate with poor breast cancer prognosis, suggesting that XIST prevents the acquisition of stem cell-like properties by maintaining XCI and limiting enhancer activation (102). Similarly, XIST knockdown in ovarian cancer cells enhances mesenchymal-like cancer stem cell populations, increasing cellular plasticity, consistent with the significant downregulation of XIST in ovarian tumors (103). Beyond cancer, Xist/XIST plays a role in sex-biased immunological differences: XIST dysregulation is observed in disease-associated atypical B cells in female-biased autoimmune disorders, such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), with impaired XIST–protein complex that maintains XCI (104). Furthermore, male mice expressing the Xist transgene develop autoantibodies and altered T- and B-cell characteristics that resemble female-like patterns, resulting in increased autoimmune severity (105).CancersLncRNAs have been extensively associated with cancers. FAL1 gene amplification and overexpression demonstrate significant associations with poor survival in ovarian cancer patients, indicating FAL1’s oncogenic function (98). FAL1 ensures the suppression of the tumor suppressor p21 and other PRC1 target genes by binding and stabilizing BMI1, a PRC1 subunit (98). HOTAIR overexpression alters a genome-wide H3K27me3 pattern through its interaction with EZH2−PRC2 complex, promoting breast cancer metastasis (106). LincRNA-p21 functions as a transcriptional coactivator for p53-mediated induction of p21 by recruiting HNRNPK in cis, thereby contributing to the p53–p21 pathway, including G1/S checkpoint regulation and cell proliferation (107). NEAT1 promotes tumorigenesis by attenuating oncogene-dependent p53 activation, thereby enhancing the survival of precancerous cells. As such, NEAT1 is recognized as a promising therapeutic target, as supported by the increased sensitivity of cancer cells to chemotherapy by NEAT1 targeting (65).Neurological and neurodevelopmental disordersIn contrast to its tumor-promoting role, NEAT1 provides neuroprotection in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), a neurodegenerative disorder. NEAT1 facilitates TDP-43 nuclear body formation through LLPS, preventing pathological cytoplasmic aggregation of TDP-43. However, this protective mechanism is compromised in ALS, in which the D169 mutation in TDP-43 disrupts NEAT1−TDP-43 interactions, resulting in toxic cytoplasmic TDP-43 accumulation and neurodegeneration (108). Notably, dysregulated lncRNAs have been identified in association with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) genetic risk (109). Lnc-NR2F1 has been identified through recurrent mutation of its locus in patients with autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability (ASD/ID). Lnc-Nr2f1’s chromatin binding sites are enriched for basic helix loop helix (bHLH) factor motifs, likely enabling it to coordinate neuronal genes with bHLH transcription factors (110). This work suggests a causal link between lncRNA mutations and neurodevelopmental disorders. As another example, Evf2 interacts with BRG1 chromatin remodeling factor and inhibits its activity, converting active enhancers to repressed enhancers. Mutations causing Coffin–Siris syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder, reside within the BRG1’s RNA binding domain, suggesting that Evf2−BRG1 interaction is important for neurodevelopment (111).Immune disorders Lnc13 harbors celiac-associated SNPs, and exhibits reduced expression in celiac disease patients. Lnc13 associates with HNRNPD and HDAC1 on chromatin to repress inflammatory gene expression. The disease-associated lnc13 variant shows reduced binding affinity for this repressive complex, resulting in compromised intestinal immune homeostasis and contributing to inflammatory responses in celiac disease (112). NeST interacts with WDR5, which increases H3K4me3 at the IFN-γ locus, resulting in enhanced IFN-γ production. This mechanism controls susceptibility to viral and bacterial pathogens, suggesting NeST dysregulation may influence infectious disease outcomes (113).CONCLUSION AND PERSPECTIVESOur view of lncRNAs has shifted considerably. Rather than transcriptional noise, they actively recruit chromatin modifiers, form nuclear bodies, and mediate long-range genomic interactions. LncRNAs exhibit pronounced species-, tissue-, and cell-state-specificity, enabling context-dependent regulation that underlies both normal physiology and pathogenesis.Advanced RNA-centric technologies are key to understanding lncRNA biology at the mechanistic level. Combined with 3D genome mapping approaches, these methods have deepened our understanding of how lncRNAs modulate genome topology and integrate regulatory pathways across chromatin states, cofactor interactions, and nuclear architecture to orchestrate complex gene regulation. Deciphering the lncRNA interactome at higher spatiotemporal resolution and across diverse biological contexts will be essential to identify key mechanisms and functions. This will require integrative multi-omics approaches and controlled perturbation studies to establish causal links to function.LncRNAs have emerged as therapeutic targets. Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) that degrade Ube3a-ATS can reactivate the healthy paternal Ube3a allele in Angelman syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder (114). Small molecules that bind Xist’s RepA motif can block XCI and reactivate the Xi containing a healthy allele, illustrating how drug-like compounds can modulate lncRNA structure and function (115). Continued exploration of their molecular mechanisms and pathological roles holds promise for novel diagnostic and therapeutic strategies, particularly in complex diseases.
